# Community PS2 rosters, 1970-2022: what the saves hold

Source label for everything below and in the JSON: **community PS2 roster (unverified)**. These are fan-made NFL 2K5 (PS2) roster saves that the user supplied as `.max` files. `build/eras/ps2max.py` unpacked them into `data/eras/ps2_saves/` (list: `data/eras/ps2_saves/unpack.log`). This page describes what the saves contain and points out obvious defects. It does not check whether players, ratings or bios are historically correct. The only historical facts used are franchise founding, move and rename dates, and each one cites a source.

- Extractor: `build/eras/extract_community_rosters.py` (run `python -I build/eras/extract_community_rosters.py --tables`; it takes about a minute)
- Output: `data/eras/community_rosters/<season>.json` (one per season), `2019-ros2019.json` (the second 2019 save) and `index.json` (every save with its quality stats, plus the franchise saves that were not extracted). About 53 MB in total.
- Parser: 2K Football Mod Tools' `RosterDocument` (`.tmp-2k-football-mod-tools/mod_editor/core/nfl2k5_roster_records.py`). Positions come from `position_names('retail')`, ratings follow `RATING_BYTE_ORDER`, and birth years are decoded with `decode_birth_year(raw, reference_year=season)`. Because that decode often gives the wrong century, a second decode is stored as well (see Birth years).
- Retail baseline: the Xbox disc's own roster (`original/vc_53450030`, read with `rr.load_image`). It is used to spot records left unchanged from retail 2004. The PS2 retail roster is assumed, not proved, to be the same.

## What was extracted

- **48 roster saves covering 47 seasons.** Each is TYPE `ROS` with a 593,984-byte payload: a version-0 `ROST` arena at +0x20 holding 52 team records. `RosterDocument` loaded every one without errors.
- **Seasons with no roster save:** 1975, 1996, 1999, 2000, 2005 and 2014. There is no franchise save for these seasons either.
- **2019 has two saves:**
  - `Ros2k19` (save directory `BASLUS-20919Ros2k19`) is the primary save and is written to `2019.json`.
  - `ros2019` (generic directory `BASLUS-209192K7`) is written to `2019-ros2019.json`.
  - Its records are much closer to the 2020 save than to `Ros2k19` (see Derived saves), so its "2019" label is doubtful.
- **Skipped:**
  - `rosfictional` (not a season).
  - `Ros1967`: named like a roster but holds a franchise payload, byte-identical to `Fra1967`.
  - All pre-1970 stems (`Ros40`, `Ros41`, `Ros43` (holds 1941), `Ros44`, 1946-1969).
  - All franchise saves (TYPE `FXG`, 720,044 bytes). They are listed in `index.json` under `franchise_saves_not_extracted`. Every 1970+ franchise season also has a roster save.
- **Integrity: every extracted save verifies.**
  - 43 saves carry the usual 4-byte CRC-32 in `EXTRA`.
  - Five (`ros1973`, `Ros1978`, `ros1989`, `ros2k10`, `ros2k12`) carry a 20-byte HMAC-SHA1 instead. It matches the key the Xbox release uses (`rr.SIG_KEY`), so these saves passed through a tool that signs the Xbox way.
  - The only mismatch in the whole set is a franchise save, `Fra41` (pre-1970, not extracted).

### JSON layout (per season)

- **`source`:** stem, save directory, payload path, sha256, TYPE and integrity.
- **`parser`:** how the payload was loaded.
- **`rating_order`:** the 28 rating names.
- **`notes`:** how to read the fields.
- **`quality`:** every number used on this page.
- **`teams`:** all 52 team records. Each has index, city, nickname, abbreviation, `player_count`, `is_club` and `kind`, plus `renamed_from_retail` (and `retail_name` when it was renamed). Club slots also carry:
  - the mean `overall_estimate`;
  - cloned-body, duplicate-player, shared-name, implausible-body and retail-match counts;
  - a position census.
- **`players`:** one line per primary-pool record that is on a team list or the free-agent list. Draft-class records (380 per save, generated by the game) and template records are left out. Each player has:
  - `first`, `last`, `group`;
  - `team` (first club slot 0-31, or null), `team_abbreviation`, `depth_slot`, `teams` (every list, including Pro Bowl and alumni);
  - `position`, `position_code`, `jersey`, `height_in`, `weight_lb`;
  - `birth_date`, `birth_date_if_age_shifted`, and the raw `birth_year_raw`, `birth_month`, `birth_day`;
  - `years_pro`, `college`;
  - `ratings` (28 values in `rating_order`), `overall_estimate` (the parser's own estimate, not the game's overall);
  - `pbp_id`, `photo_id`, `player_type`, `retail_2004_match`, `flags`.

## Club slots and team names

- **All 32 club slots hold players in every save.** Every slot has exactly 53 players, 1,696 per save. The one exception is 2012, where IND has 52.
- **The 20 non-club records (32-51: user, fantasy, alumni, Pro Bowl) are never renamed.** Their lists point at club players, as in retail.
- **45 of the 48 saves keep the retail 2004 name on every club slot, whatever the season.**
  - The 1970 save does **not** rename slots to 1970 franchises: slot 10 is still "Indianapolis Colts" (IND) and slot 21 "New England Patriots" (NE).
  - At least the Colts slot is filled by franchise lineage under its 2004 name. The 1970 "Indianapolis Colts" slot holds players named Johnny Unitas, Earl Morrall, John Mackey, Bubba Smith and Mike Curtis, all listed on the 1970 Baltimore Colts roster [S1]. The Colts moved from Baltimore to Indianapolis in 1984 [S2].
  - By the same dates, the save names are out of era for the Cardinals (St. Louis until the 1988 move to Phoenix [S2]), the Rams (Los Angeles until 1995, and again from 2016 [S2]), the Raiders (Los Angeles 1982-1994 [S2]) and the Oilers/Titans slot (Houston Oilers 1960-1996 [S7]). The saves leave all of these at their 2004 names. Likewise, the 2016-2021 saves keep "St. Louis Rams", "San Diego Chargers", "Oakland Raiders" and "Washington Redskins".
- **Only three saves rename club slots.**
  - **1978 (`Ros1978`) and 1979 (`Ros1979`), five slots:**
    - 7 Arizona Cardinals -> "StLouis Cardinals" (STL)
    - 8 San Diego Chargers -> "L Chargers" (LA)
    - 10 Indianapolis Colts -> "Baltimore Colts" (BAL)
    - 23 St. Louis Rams -> "L Rams" (LAR)
    - 25 Washington Redskins -> "Washington FB" (WAS)
  - Three of those five match the era: the Cardinals were in St. Louis (until 1988), the Colts in Baltimore (until 1984) and the Rams in Los Angeles (until 1995) [S2]. Two do not:
    - The Chargers moved back to Los Angeles only in 2017 [S2].
    - "Washington Football Team" was the name for 2020-2021 [S3].
    - These two look carried over from a modern base file. That is an inference.
  - **2022 (`Ros2k22`), four slots:**
    - 8 -> "L Chargers" (LA)
    - 22 Oakland Raiders -> "LV Raiders" (LVR); the Raiders moved to Las Vegas in 2020 [S2]
    - 23 -> "LA Rams" (LAR)
    - 25 -> "Washington FB" (WAS). The team became the Commanders in 2022 [S3], so this is the 2020-2021 name.

Founding, move and rename dates used here:

| Slot (2004 name) | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|

| CAR, JAX | Carolina Panthers and Jacksonville Jaguars started play in the 1995 season | [S4] |
| BAL (Ravens) | the Cleveland Browns moved to Baltimore as the Ravens in 1996 | [S2] |
| CLE | the Browns franchise was suspended (the team had moved to Baltimore as the Ravens in 1996 [S2]) and resumed play in 1999 | [S5] |
| HOU (Texans) | the Texans began play as an expansion team in 2002 | [S6] |
| TEN | Houston Oilers 1960-1996, Tennessee Oilers 1997-1998, Titans from 1999 | [S7] |

What the saves do with these slots:

- **No slot is ever empty.** Every not-yet-existing franchise gets a full 53-man roster.
- **In most saves those slots stand out as low-rated.** Their mean `overall_estimate` is about 40-56, against a league median of 66.6-71.5. A slot counts as low-rated when its mean is at least 12 points below the save's median (bold in the table below). The low-rated sets line up with the dates above:
  - **1970, 1971, 1973, 1974:** all six future slots (TB, SEA, CAR, JAX, BAL, HOU).
  - **1976-1994:** TB and SEA rate normally from 1976 on, except TB in 1978 (47.8). CAR, JAX, BAL and HOU stay low-rated, except in 1979 and 1993 (see the next point) and two single slots that fall short of the threshold: 1980 HOU (65.4) and 1990 JAX (59.5).
  - **1995:** CAR and JAX rate normally; BAL and HOU are low-rated.
- **In some saves the future slots are rated close to the league median,** so nothing in the data marks them as fillers:
  - 1972, 1979 and 1993 have no low-rated slot.
  - 1997: BAL, HOU, CLE and TEN are all 67.7-67.8 against a median of 72.0. 1998: HOU is 66.2 and CLE 66.0 against 69.5.
- **Cloned bodies.** Groups of differently named records share birth date, height, weight, college, position and jersey; only the name was changed. They appear in the filler slots of 1970, 1978, 1979, 1980, 1983, 1992 and 1995, typically 11-22 per slot.
  - Example, 1970 HOU: "Gary Frazier" and "Andy Hazelton" are both a DT wearing #91, 6'5", 291 lb, with the same birth date and the same college.
  - 1979 also has cloned bodies in CIN (20) and NO (16). Those franchises existed in 1979, so the save pads real clubs the same way.
- **Filler names look recombined.**
  - In the low-rated slots, 66-98% of players have a first name and a last name that each appear elsewhere in the save, but never together. In ordinary slots the share is 34-48%. The 2002 HOU copies are left out: their full names exist on PHI.
  - This suggests the names were built from other players' name parts. Examples from 1970 HOU: "Howie Unitas", "Dick Kostelnik", "Saint Latzke". This is an inference from the numbers, not a check against history.
  - 1989 is weaker (47% against 34%), partly because its BAL slot still carries retail 2004 Ravens records (see Retail leftovers).
- **2001 and 2002 Houston slot.**
  - In `Ros2k1` (2001) the HOU slot holds 52 records that duplicate PHI records: same name, same bio, same mean rating (74.8).
  - In `Ros2k2` (2002, the Texans' first season [S6]) the HOU slot holds the same 52 PHI duplicates, but with ratings near zero (mean `overall_estimate` 9.0). Neither save has a Texans roster.

## Birth years (read before using `birth_date`)

The record stores a two-digit year. Decoding it against the season, as specified for this extraction (`decode_birth_year(raw, season)`), is right only when the save author entered real birth years. Many authors instead kept players' **ages** right for the game's own 2004 calendar.

For those saves the season decode lands a century off. Example: the 1970 record named Johnny Unitas stores year 72, which decodes to 1872-05-07 against 1970, or to 1938-05-07 if the age is shifted from 2004. Neither value has been checked against history.

Each player therefore has both:

- **`birth_date`:** the season decode.
- **`birth_date_if_age_shifted`:** the year decoded against 2004, minus (2004 - season).

`quality.birth_convention` says which one the save's own `years_pro` values support. It uses shift = stored year (read against 2004) + years_pro + 22 - season:

- a median near 0 means the stored years are real birth years;
- a median near 2004 - season means the years were age-shifted.

Results:

- **Real birth years** (use `birth_date`): 2009-2013, 2018, 2019 (both saves), 2020.
- **Age-shifted to 2004** (use `birth_date_if_age_shifted`): 1971-1973, 1976, 1988-1998, 2008, 2015-2017, 2021, 2022.
- **Season too close to 2004 to tell the two apart:** 2001-2007.
- **Mixed or unclear:** 1970, 1974, 1977-1987. The median falls between the two conventions and the spread is wide; in 1982-1987 the 10th-90th percentile runs from about -13 to +27. Neither field gives trustworthy ages for these saves.

## Data-quality notes

- **Placeholder and broken names** (from `quality.name_issues`):
  - 1995: "MikeDulaney" / "20" (CHI) and "'Omar Ellison" (SD), with a stray apostrophe.
  - 1997 and 1998: a CHI player named "0" / "0".
  - 2003: "DE" / "texans" on DAL.
  - 2022: backtick apostrophes ("Adoree`", "Tre`").
  - Alternate names in brackets are typed into the name fields in 1995, 1997, 1998, 2001-2007, the second 2019 save and 2020. Examples: "Juqua Parker" / "(Thomas)", "Ezekiel (Zeke)" / "Turner".
  - Otherwise, no save has empty names or "Player"-style placeholder names on club rosters.
- **Impossible bodies:**
  - **1984-1987:** 43-69 club players per save are listed at 5'0" or 5'1" (60-61 in). 1978-1983 have smaller clusters (4-26 per save).
  - **104 in (8'8"):** 1988 (4), 1989 (7), 2001 (11); single cases in 1970, 1972-1973, 1976-1977 and 1995.
  - **Other heights:** 2001 has two players at 12 in; 1978 and 2009 have one each at 252 in; 1986, 1988 and 2015 have 0 in, and 1988 has one at 33 in.
  - **Weights of 400-405 lb** (the field's maximum) appear 1-5 times per save in the 1970s-1980s saves, plus 1998 and 2022.
- **Duplicate players:**
  - The 2001 and 2002 Houston copies of Philadelphia account for most of the 118 duplicates in each of those saves.
  - Other high counts: 1992 (44), 1989 (41), 2017 (41, spread over many teams) and 1980 (32).
  - No record sits on two club lists in any save. Duplicates are separate records.
- **Retail 2004 leftovers:**
  - **1970:** no club records, but 58 of the 235 extracted free agents are unchanged retail 2004 free agents.
  - **1989:** 31 club records match retail 2004 bios. Eighteen are in the BAL filler slot, which still holds records named, for example, Ray Lewis and Peter Boulware; the rest are in CAR (8), JAX (4) and CIN (1).
  - **1997:** 58 club records match retail 2004 bios across most teams. Some may be players whose careers spanned both years; this has not been checked.
  - **Expected matches:** 2003 (323) and 2004 (417) build on the 2004 roster. 2009-2013 also show 41-246 matches.
  - **2011 and 2017:** both carry the same 122 retail free agents (232 of their 235 extracted free agents are identical). The two files probably share a base.
- **Missing colleges:** 2001 has 189 club players without a college, and 1990-1992, 2010, 2011, 2013 and 2021 have 21-41 each. Every other save has 20 or fewer.
- **Rating scales differ by author.** League medians run from 66.6 (1978) to 77.5 (2018), and the two 2019 saves differ by 8 points (75.3 against 67.2). Ratings are not comparable across saves without rescaling.
- **Derived saves and season labels:**
  - **`Ros2k1` (2001):** its save directory is named `Ros2k2`, and it shares 1,681 of 1,696 club records (name, bio, team) with `Ros2k2` (2002). Beyond those 15 records, only the ratings differ (including the near-zero HOU ratings in 2002), so it is not an independent 2001 roster.
  - **`ros2019`:** shares 1,687 records with `Ros2k20` (2020) but only 795 with `Ros2k19`. It is most likely a variant of the 2020 file.
  - **Shared save-directory names:** `ros1977` is "Ros1976", `Ros1979` is "1978ovaR" and `ros1998` is "Ros1994". These saves are nevertheless mostly distinct in content: 2, 0 and 74 shared records with their namesakes.
  - **High overlap between adjacent saves:** 1990-1994 (628-1,289 records shared between consecutive saves) and 2010-2012 (652-791).
  - Consecutive saves never share a club record including its ratings; the only exception is a single record shared by 2001 and 2002. Authors re-rated every player.
- **Free agents and other lists:** every save has a free-agent list of 241-253 entries. 235-236 of them are extracted with group `free_agent`; the remaining 5-17 also sit on fantasy or alumni lists, so they appear with group `team` and `team` null. Every save also carries the game's 380 generated draft-class records, which are not extracted.

## Caveats

- **Thresholds are mechanical.** "Low-rated" means the slot mean is at least 12 below the save's median `overall_estimate`, which is the parser's estimate, not the game's overall. The "bad h/w" bounds are judgement calls. Nothing is changed in the data; records are only flagged.
- **Duplicates and clones are matched on stored fields only** (name, birth fields, height, weight, college, position, jersey), so two different real people with identical bios would be counted. This is very unlikely at the field widths involved.
- **Retail matching assumes the PS2 retail roster equals the Xbox disc roster.**
- **None of the rosters, ratings or bios have been verified against history.** Treat every file as community PS2 roster (unverified).

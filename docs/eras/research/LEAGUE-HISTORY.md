# My NFL Eras: league history research, 1970-2025

2026-10-15. Verified league structure for 1970-1980 and the franchise lineage events
1970-2025, for the PC-side league database (`docs/eras/PHASE1-ARCHITECTURE.md` section 4).

Data files (generated by `build/eras/league_history.py`, which reads a local cache of the
source pages in `build/eras/league/pfr_cache`; this page is written by
`build/eras/league_history_doc.py`):

* `data/eras/league/nfl_seasons_1970_1980.json`: every team each season 1970-1980 with
  city, name, conference, division, record and finish; games; playoff format; Super Bowl;
  every postseason game.
* `data/eras/league/franchise_events_1970_2025.json`: the 32 current franchises with stable
  ids, an identity timeline (city, name, conference, division by season range) and every
  expansion, relocation, rename, realignment, suspension and reactivation 1970-2025, plus
  league-wide changes (schedule length, playoff format) and a per-season summary.

## How it was verified

* **Primary source: Pro-Football-Reference (PFR)** season standings
  (`/years/<season>/`) for 1969 (NFL and AFL pages) and every season 1970-2025, and the 32
  franchise pages (`/teams/<code>/`). PFR itself answers scripts with a Cloudflare
  challenge, which was not bypassed; the pages were read from **Wayback Machine copies**
  (the snapshot URL of every page is in `pfr_snapshots` of the events file, in the season
  `sources`, and in `build/eras/league/pfr_cache/index.json`). Every
  team, name, conference, division, record and playoff game in the data is parsed from those
  pages, not typed by hand.
* Cross-checks: the team name a season page gives was compared with the name the franchise
  page gives for the same season (no differences). Wikipedia season articles agree: 1970
  and 1978 on all six divisions, games and playoff format; 1977 on all six divisions and
  games; 1971 on the AFC East and the
  Patriots' rename; 1976 on the Seattle/Tampa Bay placements, 14 games and the playoff
  format. Super Bowl dates and scores in PFR's playoff tables agree with ESPN's Super Bowl
  list. The build script asserts that every team played the same number of games each
  season 1970-1980 and that PFR's division-winner and wild-card marks match the format.
* Official cross-check, 1970-1980: the NFL's own past standings (2012 NFL Record & Fact Book,
  "Past Standings", pp. 388-393, [archived PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20130925182142/http://static.nfl.com/static/content/public/image/history/pdfs/History/Past_Standings_2011.pdf))
  agree with PFR on every team, conference, division, W-L-T record and wild card. They order
  four pairs of tied, non-playoff teams differently from PFR; the data follows the NFL there
  (`order_note` on 1974 AFC East, 1976 NFC West, 1977 AFC East and 1977 AFC Central).
  A second set of Wayback copies of the PFR 1970-1980 pages (December 2023 to January 2024
  snapshots, parsed separately) also matched the data on every field.
* Narrative facts (why and when a move happened) come from the Pro Football Hall of Fame team
  histories, NFL.com, team sites and news reports, each cited on its event. Facts with only
  a Wikipedia source are marked "Wikipedia cross-check" in the data and listed below.

Playoff format notes (in `playoff_format` of each season):

* 1970-1977: three division winners and one wild card (the best non-division winner) per
  conference, 8 teams, three rounds. 1970-1974 the home teams rotated among divisions by a
  set schedule regardless of record; the rotation was dropped in 1975 (Pro Football Hall of
  Fame).
* 1978-1980: a second wild card per conference, 10 teams; the two wild cards meet in a Wild
  Card round, the division winners have byes (Pro Football Hall of Fame). The higher-seeded
  wild card hosts (Wikipedia); PFR game sites agree where records differ. The NFL Record &
  Fact Book gives the two equal-record cases: 1978 NFC, Atlanta was first wild card over
  Philadelphia (9-7 each) on record against common opponents; 1980 AFC, Oakland was first
  wild card over Houston (11-5 each) on conference record. It also states each season's other
  tiebreaks (division order, top seed, wild-card berths); the general tiebreak procedure was
  not researched.
* A wild card could not meet its own division winner in the divisional round: the NFL Record
  & Fact Book says that from 1970 to 1989 two teams from the same division could not meet
  before the conference championship game, and every PFR divisional matchup 1970-1980 is
  consistent with it.

## Matching the historic teams on the disc

`data/eras/historic_teams_2k5.json` labels teams by the game's own nickname. To join it to
these ids: `oilers` (HOU_1961, HOU_1979) and `titans` (TEN_1999) are `oilers_titans`;
`redskins` is `washington`; `texans` on DAL_1962 is the game's label for a 1962 Dallas team
and, if it is the AFL Dallas Texans, belongs to `chiefs` (the Pro Football Hall of Fame says
Lamar Hunt moved that franchise to Kansas City in 1963 and renamed it the Chiefs); the
identity of DAL_1962 itself was not checked. `raiders` with city LA (LA_1983) and `rams`
with LA (LA_1979) both use "LA" in the game; here the 1982-1994 Raiders display as `LARD`.

## Not verified, conflicts and limits

* **PFR through the Wayback Machine.** The live PFR site was not read (Cloudflare challenge).
  The copies are recent: December 2024 for the 1970-1980 season pages, October 2025 to
  March 2026 for the other season and franchise pages (the 2025 season page is a
  2026-01-07 snapshot). The franchise pages were used only to cross-check names.
* **Conflict: order of tied teams.** PFR's standings order differs from the NFL's official
  standings for four tied, non-playoff pairs: 1974 AFC East (Jets/Patriots), 1976 NFC West
  (Saints/Falcons), 1977 AFC East (Jets/Bills), 1977 AFC Central (Oilers/Bengals). The data
  uses the NFL order (NFL Record & Fact Book; Wikipedia's season articles agree). Other ties
  in 1970-1980 are listed in the same order by both.
* **Wikipedia only:** why Seattle and Tampa Bay swapped
  conferences in 1977 (Buccaneers.com confirms the placements, not the reason); the
  Oilers' 1997 Memphis and 1998 Nashville home venues; the 1982 top-eight-per-conference
  tournament rule; the 1987 replacement-player games; the 1996 deal's legal form (the HOF
  page says only that Modell "agreed to relinquish" the name, colors and history and calls the
  1996 team "the new Baltimore Ravens").
* **Conflict:** the Hall of Fame Chargers page says the team moved back to Los Angeles "in
  2016"; PFR lists 2016 as San Diego and 2017 as Los Angeles, and NFL.com reported the move
  announcement on January 12, 2017. The data uses 2017.
* **Not covered:** stadium changes inside a home area (the Patriots' 1971 move to Foxboro is
  noted because it came with the rename), preseason, rules, overtime, schedules, the draft,
  and leagues other than the NFL (the 1969 NFL and AFL appear only as the 1970 starting point).
* **Abbreviations** (`abbreviation`) are a display convention chosen here, not historical
  data. PFR codes (`pfr_code`) are PFR's own franchise keys.
* **Ties:** PFR omits the ties column in seasons with no ties (1975, 1977, 1979, ...); those
  are recorded as 0.

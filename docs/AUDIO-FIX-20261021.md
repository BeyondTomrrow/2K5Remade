# In-game audio: pitch warble, crowd, SOFTDRINK skip (2026-10-21/22)

User report (2026-10-21): "play list doesnt work when i skip a song in softdrink plays a song cant
skip no crowd audio in both and its glitched sounds like deep voice filter and then alvin and the
chipmunks".

Evidence sheet: `logs\audio\audio-fix-sheet.png`. Clips (21 s each, the same moment -- SOFTDRINK
Main Menu, R3 pressed): `logs\audio\before-clip.wav` (two presses, the song plays on) and
`logs\audio\after-clip.wav` (one press, the next song starts).

## Results

### 1. SOFTDRINK skip

Before (run `s1`, SOFTDRINK v0.6.1, the build before the fix): three R3 presses in the Main Menu,
`[MUSIC] R3: next cribmusic track requested (now 0)` three times, never an advance; the song keeps
playing (`logs\audio\s1-skip-spec.png`, presses at 295.5 s and 307.5 s of `s1.wav`).

Cause: `nfl2k5_retail_music_take_next()` is consumed by the gen patch `LOCALMUSIC_RETAIL_NEXT` at
the top of `sub_00280620`. The only way into 0x00280620 is `jmp 0x280620` at 0x000F6505, the end of
`sub_000F6420`; SOFTDRINK's XBE differs inside `sub_000F6420`, so it runs interpreted and the
interpreter follows the jump into the retail routine's bytes -- the native C (and its hook) never
runs. SOFTDRINK also replaced the stop (0x0027FEC0 -> `jmp 0x014DFC41`) and the advance
(0x0027F040 -> `jmp 0x014DFE72`); the advance with the stop before it clears its "same song again"
flag and asks its shuffle for the next song (state 2), which is what a skip should do.

Fix (`src/nfl2k5_local_music.cpp`): an interpreter hook at 0x00280620 takes the request and returns
into the stop with the advance and 0x00280620 as return addresses (the pack's own versions run);
the second arrival restores eax/ecx/edx (as the native hook does) and lets the routine run. The
log now shows every press (`R3: next ... requested`, `R3: skip the playing song ...`,
`R3: nothing to skip ...`) and every advance.

After (run `s2`, same pack, same sandbox): three presses at 247.3 / 281.3 / 301.3 s of `s2.wav`,
three `[MUSIC] R3: the retail player advances (stop + next on the guest thread)`, a different song
after each (`logs\audio\s2-skip-spec.png`, short silences of 50-320 ms where the stream restarts).

The playlist-wins work (the player's own Jukebox playlist over SOFTDRINK's shuffle) is the music
agent's (docs/MUSIC-FIX-20261021.md); a playlist song is skipped by the existing `end_pos` path,
which is native code in both builds.

### 2. Pitch

Runs: `b1`/`b3` retail (render scale 9-12 as in the player's settings), `b2` retail at scale 1,
`s1`/`s2` SOFTDRINK v0.6.1; 5-8 minutes of CPU-vs-CPU play each (A every 6 s for the coin toss and
the play calls). `logs\audio\pitch-before.png` = the per-second view of `b1`;
`logs\audio\pitch-after.png` = per-sound drift (`b3`) + xemu comparison.

* **Who writes pitch.** `[PITCHSET]` stacks: a 3D sound's frequency is set by the game's sound
  state machine `sub_0003DBC0` (0x3DE85 -> DirectSound SetFrequency 0x004478C5) right before
  `VOICE_ON`; while it plays, DirectSound's 3D update (game calls at 0x3E19C position /
  0x3E1C5 velocity, 0x447A4B / 0x447A80) rewrites it by 1-60 units (0.3-18 cents): doppler.
  Per sound (1295 3D sounds in `b3`): median 0 c, p95 11 c, p99 29 c. The 8 larger ones are voices
  the 3D engine re-binds to a new sound without a new `VOICE_ON` (CBO jumps back).
* **Play duration.** 1361 3D one-shots in `b0`: time from `VOICE_ON` to the title's `VOICE_OFF`
  over (LBO / frequency): median 1.04 (the excess is the title's stop latency). A sound decoded at
  the wrong rate would be 0.5 or 2.
* **Commentary** (voices 0x46-0x49, centre bin) and **music** (0x44/0x45 in the menus, the
  playlist on 0x84 at 44.1 kHz): one pitch per clip, never moving (in-second spread 0).
* **The one voice that does slide** is the crowd-reaction stream (track 0, voices 0x44/0x45 in a
  match): the game sets its pitch in cents (`sub_0003D0D0`, object field +0x2150 x 1/1200 octave,
  `sub_000CF830`) between -200 c (calm, deep) and about +150 c (excited) together with its volume.
  xemu oracle (`tools\audio\xemu_freq.gdb`, a hardware breakpoint on the same SetFrequency call,
  `logs\audio\xemu-freq.log`): the title-screen crowd swell is 19644, 19941, ... 23352 Hz peak, then
  the slow decay; the port: 19642 ... 23355 Hz. Same values (the step count differs only because
  xemu ran slowed by the breakpoint); the other streams match too (12000, 16000, 22050, 48000 Hz).
  This is the original game's crowd, and the most likely thing heard as "deep, then chipmunk"
  when the crowd bed under it is quiet.
* **Pacing.** VP frames per second median 1500 (nominal 1500) in every run; voice-lock waits 0.1-
  2.3 s and ISR-stall waits 1-5 s over 10 minutes; no dropped buffers; 30-90 XAudio2 underruns per
  run, a third of them during loads. A first baseline (`b0`, with `RECOMP_APU_VOICE_LOG` on) ran
  2-4 % slow with 6-23 underruns per 10 s in play -- caused by the logging itself (fprintf under
  the APU lock); without it (`b1`..`s2`) play runs at full rate.

### Root causes (three, in the order they were found)

1. **No cushion to speak of, and nothing to rebuild it.** The APU was paced by the wall clock
   (`throttle`, 5.33 ms per 256 samples) and handed XAudio2 1024-sample (21 ms) buffers. The
   speakers consume at the same rate, so the queue stayed at whatever level the last disturbance
   left: after a pause of more than 64 ms the throttle resynchronised (time dropped) and the queue
   sat at 0-1 buffers for good -- bN: 3..4 buffers until a 107 ms pause at 56 s, then `queue=1..1`
   for minutes and underruns in seconds with full VP rate and no ISR wait.
   **Fix** (`apu_core.c` `throttle_by_queue`, `apu_xaudio2.c`): one 256-sample buffer per monitor
   frame (5.3 ms granularity, 48 deep) and the APU paced by the queue itself
   (`submitted - SamplesPlayed`): a frame runs when less than the target (56 ms,
   `RECOMP_AUDIO_TARGET_MS`) is queued and waits while more is. The APU now follows the audio
   device's clock; after any pause the queue is refilled at once. If the device stops consuming for
   250 ms the old wall-clock pacing takes over. The APU thread also runs at
   `THREAD_PRIORITY_HIGHEST` (it lost 100+ ms to busy host threads in hitches).
2. **The game thread held the guest lock while spinning on the GPU.** `[GGL] ISR thread waiting
   30 ms` sampling (`RECOMP_GGL_ISR_SAMPLE=1`, xbox_ggl.c) showed the owner at
   `sub_004262F0+0xCD0..0xCE0` in ~300 of 300 samples: D3D's wait for the push-buffer read position
   (`loc_00426427`, a pure-memory spin). With the GPU work on the render thread, a slow GPU frame
   kept the game thread in that spin with the guest lock held, so the APU's interrupt (DirectSound's
   ISR, run on the timer thread under the same lock) could not run; the APU front end stays
   trapped until it does, and after 40 ms the pipeline emits silence. That is the coupling between
   frame-rate dips and audio dropouts. **Fix**: gen patch `GPU_WAIT_PREEMPT`
   (tools/apply-gen-patches.py) -- the spin calls `xbox_ggl_preempt_point`, which hands the lock to
   a waiting interrupt as the console's interrupt would preempt the spin (costs one compare when
   nobody waits). Slow-frame test (`RECOMP_TEST_SLOW_FRAMES=20:6:45`): ISR-wait silence 706 ms ->
   61 ms, >30 ms ISR waits ~300 -> 2, underruns 2 -> 0.
3. **The test recording itself.** `[APUSLOW] output ... wav 24-60 ms`: RECOMP_AUDIO_WAV wrote to
   disk on the audio thread. Now a low-priority thread writes the WAV from the report ring. (Test
   runs only, but it caused some of the residual underruns of a1/nB/sN.)

The presentation's capture mix (theme / voice into recordings) now uses `try_lock`, so the audio
thread never waits on a theme being created or destroyed (src/nfl2k5_presentation.cpp).

Vendored hunks (external/xboxrecomp is gitignored): `docs\AUDIO-REPORT-HOTKEY-20261022-xboxrecomp.diff`.

Diagnostics kept: `[APUSLOW]` lines (a pipeline frame > 20 ms with vp/dsp/output split, an output
step > 15 ms with wav/ring/tap/state/submit split, a gap > 25 ms between output frames with the

### Crowd-reaction pitch against time (the "deep, then chipmunk" timing question)

The index-based comparison in `audio-fix-sheet.png` (ours rising later and decaying slower per
update) does not hold on a time axis. `NFL2K5_CROWD_PROBE=1` (src/main.c) logs every pitch change
of voice 0x44 with QPC; `tools\audio\xemu_crowd_clock.gdb` logs the same SetFrequency in xemu with
the guest's KeTickCount (thunk slot 0x004E3D30; the guest clock stops while gdb holds the VM).
`tools\audio\crowd_clock.py`, `logs\audio2\crowd-clock-xemu.png`:

| Title-screen swell | start | peak | half-way back down | knee (21400 Hz) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| xemu (guest clock) | 19644 Hz | 23350 Hz at 6.49 s | 10.02 s | ~10.1 s |
| NFL2K5-PC 60 fps | 19642 Hz | 23355 Hz at 6.11 s | 9.95 s | ~10.1 s |
| NFL2K5-PC, every frame 40 ms late (~23 fps) | 19642 Hz | 23323 Hz at 6.41 s | 10.29 s | ~10.2 s |

Same decay slope in all three; the peak moment varies by a few tenths of a second with when the
swell is triggered. The game advances its sound sequencing by elapsed time, not per frame: the play
clock runs 1.00 game-s per wall-s at 30-45 fps as at 60 (`tools\audio\clock_vs_fps.py`, NFL2K5_CLOCK_LOG),
and in a match with forced dips the crowd slides at 17 cents/s at 55-60 fps and 17 cents/s under
30 fps (ms4, `crowd_clock.py --slow`). So low frame rates do not make the crowd stay deep and then
rush; nothing to change there. What a dip did do was stop the whole sound pipeline (cause 2 above):
everything paused for tens to hundreds of ms and resumed -- that is fixed.

### Frame-rate dips in these runs

* Not GPU-bound: the 3D engine was 30-40 % busy at render scale 9 ([BENCH] gpu_util); the long
  frames are "render thread (draws)" (D3D11 submission on the CPU) and game-thread guest code
  (`tools\audio\hitch_causes.py`).
* The deep dips track host load. a0 (median 37 fps) ran while this work compiled the game on all
  cores and another agent's game instance was running; the same build at the same scale ran at
  median 59-60 fps with only the other game running (a1, bL, nB, fB). The earlier 10-30 fps
  stretches (`logs\audio\s1-pitch-partial.png`) were taken in the same shared conditions.
* SOFTDRINK (interpreted) runs at the same median (58-59) with somewhat more seconds under 50 fps
  (fS 111 vs fB 38 of ~700); the broadcast overlay was on in all runs.
* In a normal foreground session on a quiet PC the player would see 60 with occasional 80-250 ms
  hitches (texture uploads, new shaders, loads). Since cause 2 is fixed, such a hitch no longer
  stops the sound.
